**May we not destroy what we have and regret when we lose it. **

I often stop and think on a quiet night that most of people's regrets stem from two states: consuming them wantonly while they are there, and looking back repeatedly after they are far away.

It’s easy for us to take what we have right now for granted. A companionship, a preference, a stable time, or what you are doing. Because I felt I couldn’t leave, I neglected, perfunctory, and wasted away the precious things bit by bit. When the situation changes and the tea cools down, I suddenly realize that many things are not forever and will not stay in place waiting for us to wake up.

Possession is never a pass for squandering.

Treat everything in hand well, not with caution and fear, but with sobriety and kindness. Don't be unscrupulous just because you are familiar with it, don't destroy it at will just because it is stable. Keep your sense of proportion, be gentle, and earnestly accept every gift that comes to you. Cherish it as much as you can when you can, and treat it kindly when you can be kind. This is the best answer to "possession".

However, gatherings and separations in the world are inherently unpredictable, and many encounters are destined to only go together for one journey. Not all stories have a happy ending, and not all likes can be retained for a long time.

If fate comes to an end, if it is destined to be lost, there is no need to be stuck in the same place and blame yourself repeatedly, and there is no need to hold all your faults in your heart and suffer. If you try your best, be sincere, and take it seriously, you won't have to regret it for a long time. There may be regrets, but there is no need to let regrets trap you in the long days to come. Accepting farewell, allowing to dissipate, and letting go of dignity is also a kind of growth.

Gains and losses are the normal state of life.

When you have it, be restrained and cherish it, and don't destroy it with your own hands; when you lose it, be calm and relieved, and don't regret it for a long time. Don’t consume the present, don’t dwell on the past, and put the focus back on the present.

I don’t want everything to be perfect, but I want to look back with a clear conscience.
